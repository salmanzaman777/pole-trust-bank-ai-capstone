## 1. Purpose and scope-management approach

This plan converts the sponsor-approved high-level charter into a controlled, verifiable scope baseline for the selected Pair 5 programme. It defines what will be delivered, what will not be delivered, how the work is decomposed, how acceptance will be evidenced and how proposed changes will be evaluated and authorized.

Salman Zaman is the working Project/Program Manager and custodian of this baseline. This permits coordination, drafting, traceability, evidence preparation and escalation; it does not create an unstated spending, procurement, staffing or execution limit.

Waterfall governance controls the scope baseline, phase funding, risk acceptance and exit gates. Within each authorized phase, Agile squads may refine stories and solution detail without changing the approved objective, deliverable boundary, exclusion, KPI, budget envelope or roadmap. A product backlog is not authority to expand the project scope.

## 2. Product and project scope

### Product scope

A governed enterprise AI ecosystem consisting of an approved AI platform and control foundation plus four human-accountable MVPs: an internal productivity copilot, a customer digital assistant, lending document-completeness and risk-summary support, and fraud-alert prioritization/investigator assistance.

### Project scope

The programme includes initiation and planning baselines, governance and architecture, vendor/data/security readiness, MVP design and delivery, controlled pilots, training and change adoption, benefits measurement, evidence-based scale decisions, transition to operations and closure across the approved 24-month roadmap.

## 3. Scope boundaries

### In scope

1. Enterprise AI governance framework, acceptable-use controls, model oversight and stage-gate evidence.
2. Approved enterprise AI platform architecture and API integration layer.
3. Data classification, masking, access control, retention, lineage and quality controls required by the MVPs.
4. Internal productivity copilot MVP using approved internal knowledge, prompt templates and human verification.
5. Customer digital assistant MVP for approved service inquiries, intelligent intake and designed human handoff.
6. Lending-support MVP limited to document completeness and risk summaries for human-authorized decisions.
7. Fraud-support MVP limited to alert prioritization and investigator assistance.
8. Logging, audit trails, model monitoring, fallback, incident and support arrangements required for the pilots.
9. Role-based training, communications, AI champions, office hours and adoption measurement.
10. Baselines, benefit attribution, pilot measurement, operations transition and programme closure.

### Out of scope

1. Autonomous loan, credit or pricing decisions.
2. Unsupervised financial advice or unapproved regulated communications.
3. Automatic account blocking, freezing or other adverse customer action.
4. Replacement of core banking, CRM, loan-origination or fraud case-management platforms.
5. Production scale for any use case that has not passed its security, privacy, compliance, value and adoption gate.
6. Unapproved collection or reuse of personal data, opaque model operation or removal of human accountability.
7. Expansion to new AI use cases, regions or business units without change approval.
8. Commitment of the full USD 27.5M five-year TCO as an immediate spend.
9. Vendor or product selection before sourcing, architecture, security and third-party-risk evidence and Sponsor approval.

## 8. Scope validation and change control

### Validation

1. The responsible Product Owner assembles acceptance evidence.
2. Quality, Security, Privacy, Risk and Compliance review the evidence applicable to the deliverable.
3. The Project Manager confirms traceability to the scope baseline, WBS, schedule, cost and acceptance criteria.
4. The designated functional evidence owner records validate, validate with conditions or not validated, with reasons.
5. The Sponsor records the consolidated decision to stop, remediate, continue, scale or close.

### Change-control workflow

1. Log a change request with rationale, requestor and affected baseline item.
2. Triage for completeness and urgency; an Agile backlog item alone is not a change authorization.
3. Assess effects on objectives, deliverables, exclusions, WBS, schedule, cost/TCO, benefits, quality, resources, stakeholders, security, privacy, compliance and risk.
4. Obtain specialist review evidence where a control or regulatory boundary is affected.
5. Route the decision to the Sponsor, or an expressly delegated authority, and record conditions.
6. Update all affected baselines, backlog items, registers, communications and acceptance evidence.
7. Verify the implemented change and close the request.

Until PM delegation and change thresholds are approved, every baseline change routes to the Sponsor. Any proposal for autonomous credit decisions, financial advice, automatic account action, a new use case, material KPI change, roadmap extension or TCO-envelope change requires formal Sponsor approval and, where material, an amended Business Case and Charter.

### Project/Program Manager working-role boundary

| Permitted working responsibility | Reserved until formal Sponsor delegation or evidence-backed Sponsor decision |
|---|---|
| Maintain draft planning baselines, traceability and registers | Commit or release funds |
| Convene workshops and coordinate designated functions | Engage or contract a vendor |
| Prepare impact assessments, recommendations and gate packs | Direct bank staff outside approved assignments |
| Record decisions, evidence, issues and escalations | Approve material scope, KPI, roadmap or TCO changes |
| Coordinate acceptance evidence and report status | Accept regulatory, privacy, security, model or residual risk |
| Recommend stop, remediate, continue, scale or close | Authorize a pilot, production release or scale decision unless expressly delegated |
